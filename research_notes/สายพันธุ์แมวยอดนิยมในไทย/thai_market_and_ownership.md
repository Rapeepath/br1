# Thai market for pedigree kittens, and the cost and duties of owning a cat in Thailand

Research date: 2026-10-23. Prices are in THB. Most Thai "price guide" articles reuse the same old figures (2019–2021). The newest price evidence is (a) live marketplace listings on ENNXO, checked 2026-10 (listing dates are not shown, so treat them as current asking prices of unknown age), (b) cattery websites that state prices now, and (c) a few 2025 Thai articles. Each finding says which kind of source it comes from.

## Q1. What is the typical asking price in THB for a pet-quality kitten of each breed, and what pushes the price up?

### Takeaway
Thai asking prices form two layers. Marketplace listings, which are often sold without papers, sit in the low thousands to low tens of thousands of baht. Registered catteries with pedigree papers (CFA/TICA/WCF), genetic tests and contracts charge several tens of thousands of baht, and Bengals from registered catteries start at 70,000–80,000. For every breed the range is wide, so the website should show ranges, not single prices.

**Other price evidence and price drivers:**
- Kapook says unregistered cats sell in the thousands. Cats registered with world federations (CFA, WCF, TICA) can reach six figures, and Grand Champion or world-class show titles can push prices toward millions. Breeding certification and negotiation also affect price ([Kapook, 2021-03-29](https://pet.kapook.com/view239194.html)).
- Khao Manee kittens with odd eyes (one blue, one yellow) sell for more, and international buyers seek the breed. The same farm sells Khongja (black Thai cat) kittens at 10,000 each ([LiveToLife, 2025-09-30](https://www.livetolife.com/lifestyle/trends/thai-lucky-cat)).
- A Lemon8 post on Maine Coons warns that "starting" prices around 15,000 may be mixed-breed cats, while well-typed cats cost about 30,000–50,000 and certificated cats run into six figures (seen only as a search snippet, page not fully retrieved; [Lemon8](https://www.lemon8-app.com/experience/%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%97-%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B2-%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%B9%E0%B8%99?region=th)).
- An older British Shorthair cattery page says imported cats cost 55,000–90,000 depending on lineage and age. This is only a search snippet, and the page is from 2016 ([Kunlek Farm](https://kunlekfarm.wordpress.com/2016/11/09/featured-content-2/)).
- Bangkok Bengal Cats sells breeding/show kittens only to registered catteries and does not list their prices. It takes a 15,000 waitlist deposit and then 50% of the price to reserve ([Bangkok Bengal Cats](https://www.bangkokbengalcats.com/adoption-pricing/)).
- The ENNXO marketplace shows "แบ่งเลี้ยง" (co-ownership, where the breeder keeps breeding rights) at well below the normal price, for example a 6,700 Ragdoll ([ENNXO Ragdoll](https://www.ennxo.com/en/pet/cat/ragdoll); [ENNXO BSH](https://www.ennxo.com/en/pet/cat/british-shorthair)).
- A 2025 Lemon8 post from a pet shop (PONPON Petshop, 2025-05-18) says Thai breeds start in the thousands and can reach tens of thousands depending on lineage. It says foreign breeds (BSH, Scottish Fold, Bengal, Ragdoll, Maine Coon) run from tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands, and that "price isn't the only quality indicator" ([Lemon8](https://www.lemon8-app.com/@ponponpetshop30/7505744788596408840?region=th)).
- On 2025-11-18 the Thai Cabinet declared five Thai cat breeds a "national identity in the pet animal category": Wichienmaat, Suphalak, Si Sawat/Korat, Khongja and Khao Manee. The aims are conservation, protecting the breeds as intellectual property, and soft power. The article mentions no sale or export restrictions ([Time Out Bangkok, 2025-11-19](https://www.timeout.com/bangkok/th/news/five-thai-cat-breeds-now-official-national-treasures-111925)).

### Inferences
- The low ENNXO clusters (for example Scottish Fold at about 4,000, Persian at about 2,500 and American Shorthair at about 2,500) are probably kittens without registry papers. Pedigreed kittens from registered catteries usually cost several times more. The JSON ranges therefore start near the marketplace cluster and stop at a typical papered pet-quality ceiling. Top show prices (100,000+) are left out except where the sources treat them as typical (Persian and Exotic).
- Thai breeds (Siamese and Korat) are the cheapest pedigree-type kittens. Khao Manee is the exception: odd-eyed kittens from known farms sell at foreign-breed prices.
- The Cabinet's 2025 recognition may push prices of Thai breeds up, but I found no data on that yet.

### Gaps
- None of the major Thai price articles has 2025–2026 per-breed figures. The widely copied tables come from 2019–2021 (Redprice, PPTV, Kapook) or 2024 (Yora). The Lemon8 page "ราคาแมวแต่ละสายพันธุ์ในไทย 2026" would not load.
- ENNXO pages do not show listing dates. The Kaidee category page returned no listings. Priceza pages were not checked.
- I found no Thai cattery stating current prices for Persian, Exotic, BSH, Scottish Fold, Ragdoll, Maine Coon, Munchkin or American Shorthair (canimal-cattery.com would not resolve). Exotic Shorthair evidence is very thin (4 listings).
- There is no evidence on how much rare colors (for example BSH golden/chinchilla, Ragdoll mink) raise Thai prices. Kapook mentions coat appearance only in general terms.

## Q2. What should a buyer receive from a reputable Thai cattery?

### Takeaway
Reputable Thai catteries hand kittens over at 12 weeks or older, usually at 3–4 months. The kitten comes with registry pedigree papers, a vaccination record, a microchip, a written contract and a health or genetic guarantee. Marketplace sellers often offer only "vaccinated" and an FIV/FeLV-test guarantee.

### Cited Findings
- BangkokCats (Bengal, Abyssinian, Somali) is registered with CFA, TICA and WCF. The price includes vaccinations, microchip, genetic health guarantees (PK-Def and PRA-b for life, HCM for 2 years), pedigree, purchase contract and neutering. Kittens go to Thai homes at about 3–4 months (12 weeks minimum), abroad at 16 weeks or older, and at 15 weeks or older if neutered ([BangkokCats](https://bangkokcatscattery.com/)).
- Bangkok Bengal Cats keeps kittens with their mother and siblings "until they are at least three months old", or about 4 months if they are desexed at the cattery. The price includes vaccinations, microchip, pedigree, contract and genetic guarantees ([Bangkok Bengal Cats](https://www.bangkokbengalcats.com/adoption-pricing/)).
- Thai marketplace listings often advertise "วัคซีนแล้ว" (vaccinated), "รับประกันเอดส์แมว" (FIV guarantee), free panleukopenia testing, FeLV testing, and "ใบ pat/ใบเพ็ดดีกรี" (pedigree), some with WCF papers ([ENNXO Siamese](https://www.ennxo.com/en/pet/cat/siamese); [ENNXO Korat](https://www.ennxo.com/en/pet/cat/korat); [ENNXO BSH](https://www.ennxo.com/en/pet/cat/british-shorthair); [ENNXO Maine Coon](https://www.ennxo.com/en/pet/cat/maine-coon)).
- Some marketplace sellers list kittens from about 50 days or 2 months old, younger than the 12 weeks catteries use. Examples are a 50-day Khao Manee and "2 months, ready to move" Munchkin and Korat listings ([108dog Korat](https://108dog.com/marketpet/245720); [ENNXO Munchkin](https://www.ennxo.com/en/pet/cat/munchkin); a Khao Manee 50-day listing appeared in search results for [108dog](https://108dog.com/cat)).
- In Bangkok, a cat must be microchipped and registered within 120 days of birth or within 30 days of arriving in Bangkok ([The Standard](https://thestandard.co/bangkok-new-pet-rules-jan-69/); [Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/1212023)).

### Inferences
- A kitten sold at 8–10 weeks will not have finished the WSAVA core series, because the last dose must be given at 16 weeks or older. Buyers will pay for the remaining doses themselves. The 12-week minimum also matches what the reputable catteries above practise.
- Because of the Bangkok rules, a microchip number on the paperwork now matters to Bangkok buyers.

### Gaps
- I found no Thai government or veterinary-association standard for the minimum sale age of kittens. The 12-week figure comes from cattery practice, not law.
- I did not check whether Thailand has a national cat registry for pedigrees (for example a Thai cat association). International registries (CFA, TICA, WCF) are the ones Thai sellers cite.

## Q3. What kitten vaccination schedule do Thai vets use, and is rabies vaccination of cats legally required?

### Takeaway
Thai hospitals follow a schedule close to WSAVA's. Core combination vaccine (FPV/FHV-1/FCV) starts at 6–8 weeks and repeats every 3–4 weeks until 16 weeks or older. Rabies vaccine starts at about 12–16 weeks. FeLV is added for at-risk cats, rabies is boosted every year, and WSAVA 2024 adds a booster at 26 weeks. Cats are "controlled animals" under the Rabies Act B.E. 2535 (added by ministerial regulation in 2017), so owners must have them vaccinated against rabies. Bangkok also requires microchip and registration from 2026-01-10.

### Cited Findings
- WSAVA 2024 lists the core cat vaccines as FPV, FCV and FHV. "In areas of the world where rabies is endemic, vaccination against rabies virus should be considered essential for both dogs and cats (i.e. rabies vaccines are core in those places), even if there is no legal requirement" ([WSAVA 2024](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024-Guidelines-for-the-Vaccination-of-Dogs-and-Cats.pdf)).
- WSAVA 2024 says to give multiple core doses "every 2-4 weeks, with the final dose being delivered at 16 weeks of age or older". It advises revaccination "at or after 26 weeks of age (rather than waiting until 12-16 months)". If only one dose is affordable, give it at 16 weeks or older ([WSAVA 2024](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024-Guidelines-for-the-Vaccination-of-Dogs-and-Cats.pdf)).
- WSAVA 2024 says that where FeLV is endemic, FeLV vaccine is core for cats under 1 year and for adult cats with outdoor access or that live with such cats. Core vaccines "should not be given any more frequently than necessary" in adults, and an annual health check is encouraged ([WSAVA 2024](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024-Guidelines-for-the-Vaccination-of-Dogs-and-Cats.pdf)).
- Thonglor Pet Hospital (Bangkok, 24-hour) gives this schedule: FVRCP #1 at 8 weeks or later; FVRCP #2 and FeLV #1 at 12–14 weeks; FVRCP #3 and FeLV #2 at 16 weeks; rabies #1 after 16 weeks; FVRCP and rabies boosters every year. It calls rabies vaccination legally mandatory under the Rabies Act B.E. 2535, and lists FIV vaccine as "not recommended" ([Thonglor Pet](https://thonglorpet.com/petdiary/vaccines-every-cat-needs)).
- TTB bank (2025-10-29) lists: 5-disease combination from 8 weeks, 3 doses at 350–800 THB each; rabies from 14 weeks at 150–500 per dose, then yearly; distemper/cold combination from 2 months, 3 doses at 400–700; FeLV from 16 weeks, 2 doses at 600–800 ([TTB](https://www.ttbbank.com/th/fin-tips/detail/pl-costs-of-owning-a-dog)).
- PetTofu (2026-03, updated 2026-06, citing WSAVA 2024) lists: FVRCP and FeLV at 6–8 weeks; boosters at 10–12 weeks; FVRCP #3 and rabies #1 at 14–16 weeks; FVRCP booster at about 26 weeks; FVRCP and rabies at 1 year; then FVRCP every 3 years and rabies every 1–3 years depending on the product. Prices are FVRCP 250–500, rabies 200–500 and FeLV 400–700, or about 1,750–3,900 for the kitten series excluding exam fees ([PetTofu](https://pettofu.com/cat-vaccine-schedule/)).
- HDmall (2024-09-26, no vet author) says the first vaccine is at 6–8 weeks and that each cat's plan is set by the vet ([HDmall](https://hdmall.co.th/blog/c/cat-vaccination/)).
- Thairath (2018-03-20) says the Department of Livestock Development declared cats "controlled animals" under the Rabies Act B.E. 2535 in 2017. Owners must get a vet to vaccinate, keep the certificate, put an identification tag on the animal, and report suspected rabies or bites within 24 hours. For dogs, the first rabies vaccine is due at 2–4 months, with later doses per the certificate. The article lists penalties of up to 200 THB for minor violations (tags, replacement documents) and up to 3 months in prison and/or 3,000 THB for serious ones (not reporting, improper disposal) ([Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/news/local/1232716)).
- A search summary also says "cat became a controlled animal by ministerial regulation in 2017" and that the Act's Section 5 requires owners of controlled animals to have every animal vaccinated by a vet. This comes from the search tool summary of [Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/blogs/columnist/120648) and others, and the page itself was not opened.
- The Bangkok ordinance on keeping and releasing animals (B.E. 2567) was published in the Royal Gazette on 2025-01-15 and takes effect on 2026-01-10. Owners must microchip and register cats within 120 days of birth or within 30 days of bringing them into Bangkok ([The Standard, 2025-05-02](https://thestandard.co/bangkok-new-pet-rules-jan-69/); [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/351504)).
- Bangkok registration is free at 8 BMA veterinary clinics (private clinics charge their own fees) and can be done online at petregis.bangkok.go.th. Pets kept before 2026-01-10 had to be registered by 2026-04-09 and are exempt from the number limits. Limits: a condo under 80 sq.m. may keep 1 pet, a condo of 80 sq.m. or more 2, land under 20 sq.wa 2, and land over 100 sq.wa up to 6 ([Bangkok Biz News, 2026-10-23](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/1212023); [The Standard](https://thestandard.co/bangkok-new-pet-rules-jan-69/)). Fines go up to 25,000 THB under the Public Health Act B.E. 2535 ([The Standard](https://thestandard.co/bangkok-new-pet-rules-jan-69/)).
- Other local authorities issue their own rabies-control ordinances under the Act, for example the Khlong Ri Subdistrict ordinance in the Royal Gazette ([Royal Gazette](https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/141D308S0000000000500.pdf)).
- Wildlife law: in 2018 a Koh Lanta man was caught breeding a protected leopard cat (แมวดาว) with a Bengal. Officials sterilized all 17 hybrid kittens, and he was charged under the wildlife protection law for possessing protected wildlife ([The Thaiger, 2018-12-11](https://thethaiger.com/th/news/120624/)).

### Inferences
- In practice, Thai buyers should expect 3 core combination doses, the last at 16 weeks or older; rabies at 3–4 months and then every year; FeLV if the cat goes outdoors or lives with many cats; and, if the vet follows WSAVA 2024, a booster at 26 weeks.
- The 2–4 month first-rabies-dose rule is stated for dogs in the Thairath source. Since cats are now controlled animals, the same owner duty very likely applies, but I could not confirm the exact text of the ministerial regulation for cats.
- Bengal buyers should buy only domestic-generation Bengals with pedigree papers, because owning a leopard cat, or breeding with one, breaks Thai wildlife law.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Rabies Act text or the 2017 ministerial regulation directly. Krisdika, Wikisource and the PDFs could not be read, so the exact section numbers and cat-specific age rule are unconfirmed.
- I found no vaccination schedule published by the Kasetsart or Chulalongkorn university hospitals, or by the Thai Veterinary Medical Association. The schedules above come from private hospitals and WSAVA.
- I did not find the exact fine under the Rabies Act for failing to vaccinate. The 200 / 3,000 THB figures are from a 2018 news summary.

## Q4. What does it realistically cost per month and in the first year to own a cat in Thailand?

### Takeaway
Basic monthly costs run about 550–950 THB on a budget and 2,700–4,750 THB at the premium end (food, litter, parasite prevention, optional insurance). The first year typically totals about 15,000–56,000 THB, or about 15,000–30,000 on a moderate budget, before the kitten's purchase price. Sterilization costs about 950–1,900 at university hospitals and more at private hospitals. Pet insurance runs about 599–3,000 THB a year for basic plans.

### Cited Findings
- PetTofu (2026-04, updated 2026-06) gives monthly costs per cat: dry food 200–400 (economy), 500–1,000 (mid-range) or 1,000–2,000 (premium); wet food and treats 0–600; litter 80–560; flea and tick prevention 150–350; optional insurance 170–670. Monthly totals are about 550–950 (economy) to 2,700–4,750 (premium) ([PetTofu](https://pettofu.com/cat-cost-per-month/)).
- PetTofu's one-time and first-year costs: supplies 1,500–5,000; kitten vaccines (3–4 doses) 2,000–5,000; sterilization 1,500–8,000; adult annual vaccine 500–1,000; deworming and prevention 1,800–4,200 per year. First year totals 15,800–56,400, and later years 11,300–39,900 ([PetTofu](https://pettofu.com/cat-cost-per-month/)).
- TTB (2025-10-29) puts cat food at 1,500–3,000 a month (dry food 200–500 per kg, wet food 25–45 per pouch), supplies at 2,000–5,000, and sterilization at 1,500–2,500 for females and 800–1,500 for males ([TTB](https://www.ttbbank.com/th/fin-tips/detail/pl-costs-of-owning-a-dog)).
- Thairath (2025-12-16, citing TTB Analytics, Kasikorn Research and Dhipaya Insurance) gives food and treats at 1,000–3,000 a month. Care services such as bathing and vaccines cost 150–3,000 per visit, treating a general infection 500–1,500, an X-ray 800–1,500 and an ultrasound 1,500–2,500. Cats live 10–15 years ([Thairath Money](https://www.thairath.co.th/money/personal_finance/finance_banking/2902292)).
- MAKE by KBank (2022-08-20) gives food at 500–1,500 and litter at 300–500 a month, starter supplies at 650–2,600, yearly vaccines, check-ups, sterilization and parasite control together at about 2,500–5,500, grooming at 300–1,000 a visit (800–2,500 for long-haired breeds), an emergency reserve of 5,000–10,000, and a yearly total of 15,000–30,000 ([MAKE by KBank](https://makebykbank.kbtg.tech/en/articles/Cost-of-raising-cat)).
- Shopee's blog lists cat food at 300–700 a month, treats 200–500, a litter box 100–400, litter 80–200, bowls 100–400, a bed 200–1,000 and a scratching post 400–700. An automatic litter box costs about 7,000 (undated; [Shopee](https://shopee.co.th/blog/prepare-before-having-cats/amp/)).
- University hospital sterilization prices (2025-07-12): Kasetsart (Tippimarn, Pak Chong) 1,300 male and 1,500 female; Chiang Mai University (Mae On) 1,400 male and 1,700–1,900 female; Rajamangala Tawan-OK (Bang Phra) 950 male and 1,300 female, with blood tests and medication extra. A pre-anesthetic blood test costs about 500–1,000 ([The Animals Society](https://www.the-animals-society-thailand.com/post/cat-neutering-cost-detail)).
- Pet insurance (PetHub, 2026-08-25): Muang Thai Pet Chill 629 a year (accident cover 5,000); Muang Thai Cats & Dogs Plus from 3,000 (5,000 each for accident, illness and skin); TIP Pet Buddy about 1,950 (cover 10,000); TIP Pet Lover about 1,900; TTB Pet Buddy 3,000 (accident 30,000, illness 15,000); Falcon Pet Protect Sure 599 (cover 15,000). Premiums vary by breed, age and weight ([PetHub](https://www.pethub.in.th/blog/pet-insurance-thailand-2026/)).
- Muang Thai Cats & Dogs Plus plans for microchipped cats cost 3,000, 5,200, 8,100 or 12,000 a year, and Dhipaya's smallest plan (XS) starts at 500 a year (search summary of [Muang Thai](https://www.muangthaiinsurance.com/th/product/miscellaneous-insurance/Cats-Dogs-Plus) and [Checkraka](https://www.checkraka.com/personalloan/article/129546/); pages not opened).
- Bangkok microchipping and registration is free at BMA clinics ([Bangkok Biz News](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/lifestyle/1212023)).

### Inferences
- A realistic budget for the website: 1,000–3,000 THB a month for one indoor cat, plus 5,000–15,000 THB of one-off first-year costs (supplies, kitten vaccines, sterilization with blood test), plus an emergency reserve. Long-haired breeds (Persian, Maine Coon, Ragdoll) add grooming costs.
- University and NGO sterilization costs about half to one-third of private hospital prices.

### Gaps
- I found no single, dated source for the price of a routine annual check-up exam. It appears only bundled with other costs (KBank 2022) or as an unattributed figure in search snippets.
- I found no source on private-clinic microchip prices outside the free BMA clinics.

## Q5. What adoption options exist in Thailand as an alternative to buying?

### Takeaway
Several established groups rehome rescued cats for free or at low cost, usually already sterilized and vaccinated. Examples are Soi Dog Foundation (Phuket), Rak Maw Foundation (มูลนิธิรักษ์แมว, active as of 2026-09) and Lanta Animal Welfare (Koh Lanta, Krabi).

### Cited Findings
- Soi Dog Foundation, based in Phuket, rehomes cats. To adopt, email adoptions@soidog.org. It gives priority to local adoptions in Thailand, although many cats can also fly to the USA, UK and Europe ([Soi Dog – Adopt a cat](https://www.soidog.org/adopt-a-cat)).
- Soi Dog charges "No adoption fee", and "All animals are spayed/neutered, fully vaccinated and microchipped". Overseas adopters pay for the crate, flights, ground transport and rabies titer test ([Soi Dog – Adoption](https://www.soidog.org/content/adoption)). The Gill Dalley Sanctuary in Phuket holds 1,800+ dogs and cats (search summary of [Soi Dog](https://www.soidog.org/adopt-a-cat)).
- Rak Maw Foundation (มูลนิธิรักษ์แมว) began as a project in 2007 and became a foundation in 2017. It provides medical care and sterilization and rehomes cats. Its site shows adoptable cats and was updated in September 2026 ([Rak Maw](https://www.rakmaw.com/)). A 2020 Kaidee article says adoption is free, the cats are sterilized and vaccinated, and the group has rehomed more than 10,000 cats ([Kaidee blog, 2020-12-28](https://blog.kaidee.com/5-places-adopt-dogsand-cats-free/)).
- Lanta Animal Welfare (Koh Lanta, Krabi; registered Thai charity) sterilizes, vaccinates, treats and shelters stray dogs and cats and has a cat-adoption section ([Lanta Animal Welfare](https://lantaanimalwelfare.com/); [SPCA International](https://www.spcai.org/?p=9278)).
- The 2020 Kaidee article also lists Catster by Kingdom of Tigers (cat-café adoption with health checks, vaccines and blood tests), Thailand Adopter Club (Facebook) and Kaidee's free "แจกฟรี" pet listings ([Kaidee blog](https://blog.kaidee.com/5-places-adopt-dogsand-cats-free/)).
- Rescue Paws (Hua Hin) also handles adoptions (search summary of [Idealist](https://www.idealist.org/en/nonprofit/5906cc9317aa4b77aadcec1243ceed87-rescue-paws-thailand-tambon-nong-kae); not verified further).

### Inferences
- An adopted cat comes already sterilized, vaccinated and microchipped. That removes about 3,000–8,000 THB of first-year costs and meets the Bangkok microchip rule.

### Gaps
- I did not check whether Catster and Thailand Adopter Club (from 2020) are still active, so they are left out of market.json.
- I found no BMA or government shelter that rehomes cats (BMA shelters mostly take dogs; not researched in depth).
- Rak Maw's and Lanta's adoption fees and conditions were not stated on the pages I read.
